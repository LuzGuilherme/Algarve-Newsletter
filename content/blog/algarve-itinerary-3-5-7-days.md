# Algarve Itinerary: How to Spend 3, 5, or 7 Days in Southern Portugal

Planning a trip to the Algarve and wondering how to fit it all in? You're in the right place. Whether you have a long weekend or a full week, this guide breaks down exactly where to go, what to do, and — just as importantly — what to skip.

![Dramatic golden cliffs and turquoise water at Praia da Marinha, one of Europe's most beautiful beaches](/images/beaches/Praia-da-Marinha.jpg)

After years of living in this corner of Portugal, we've refined these itineraries through countless trips with visiting friends and family. These aren't the routes you'll find in generic guidebooks — they're built around timing hacks, local restaurants, and the kind of insider knowledge that turns a good trip into an unforgettable one.

Here's how to see the best of the Algarve in 3, 5, or 7 days.

## Before You Start: Essential Logistics

### Getting Around

A **rental car is essential** for these itineraries. Public transport exists but is too slow and infrequent to cover this ground. Pick up your car at Faro Airport — all major routes start from here.

**Key driving facts:**
- The **A22 motorway** runs the entire length of the coast
- **Toll roads:** Register your license plate at portagemonline.pt or rent a Via Verde device with your car to avoid fines
- **Speed limits:** 120 km/h on motorways, 90 km/h on national roads, 50 km/h in towns
- **Fuel:** Cheapest at supermarkets (Continente, Intermarché). Fill up before heading to remote areas

### When to Visit

- **Best months:** May, June, September — warm weather, manageable crowds, reasonable prices
- **July-August:** Hot, crowded, and expensive — but the sea is warmest
- **October-April:** Mild weather, empty beaches, lower prices. Perfect for hiking and sightseeing, less so for swimming
- **Water temperature reality:** Cold until late June (16-18°C). Comfortable July-September. Warmest in September (22-24°C)

### Which Itinerary Is Right for You?

- **3 days:** The essential highlights — dramatic cliffs, iconic caves, and the edge of Europe. You'll see the Algarve's greatest hits but at a faster pace.
- **5 days:** A balanced trip that adds the elegant eastern Algarve and the mountain interior. Enough time to slow down and enjoy long lunches.
- **7 days:** The definitive Algarve experience. Everything above plus the wild west coast, Ria Formosa islands, and time to actually relax.

## The 3-Day Algarve Itinerary: The Essential Highlights

This is for travellers who want to see the Algarve's most dramatic landscapes in a long weekend. It's focused on the western and central coast — the most visually spectacular stretch.

### Day 1: Lagos — Golden Cliffs and Old-Town Charm

**Base: Lagos**

Start your Algarve trip in Lagos, the region's most perfectly positioned town. It combines dramatic cliff beaches, a charming old town, and the best restaurant scene on the western coast.

![The charming town of Lagos with its marina and historic old town](/images/towns/lagos.jpg)

**Morning: Ponta da Piedade at Sunrise**

This is the single best way to start an Algarve trip. Drive to Ponta da Piedade and arrive by **7:30am** — you'll have the Algarve's most iconic rock formations almost entirely to yourself.

The towering golden sea stacks, natural arches, and turquoise grottoes are extraordinary at first light. Walk along the clifftop path, descend the stairs to the lighthouse viewing platform, and take it all in before the tour boats arrive at 10am.

**Mid-morning: Kayak or Boat Tour (2 hours)**

Book a kayak or boat tour departing from Lagos Marina at 9:30-10am. You'll paddle through the sea arches and grottoes of Ponta da Piedade from water level — a completely different perspective from the clifftop. Guided kayak tours cost €35-50 per person and last about 2 hours.

**Lunch: Lagos Old Town**

Head into the old town for lunch. Our picks:

- **Dom Sebastião** — An institution since 1979. Try the cataplana de amêijoas (clam stew). Ask for the upstairs dining room — it's quieter and has more character. Reservations recommended.
- **A Tasca do Kiko** — Tiny tapas spot near the marina. The garlic prawns and tuna tataki are outstanding. Single-page menu that changes with the seasons.

After lunch, wander the cobblestone streets inside the medieval walls. Follow the old town walls walk for panoramic views over the marina and rooftops.

**Afternoon: Praia do Camilo and Praia Dona Ana**

Walk south from the old town to the Algarve's two most famous cove beaches.

![Golden cliffs framing turquoise water at Praia do Camilo in Lagos](/images/beaches/Praia-do-Camilo.jpg)

**Praia do Camilo** — 200 wooden steps down to a cliff-framed double cove. At low tide, a rock tunnel connects to a second, emptier section. Go before 3pm for the best light.

**Praia Dona Ana** — Lagos' postcard beach with full facilities, turquoise water, and spectacular cliff scenery. Better for a longer beach session.

**Evening: Sunset and Dinner**

Watch sunset from the Ponta da Piedade clifftop (arrive by 7pm in summer) or take a sunset sailing cruise from the marina (€30-55 per person, drinks included — choose a small sailboat over a large catamaran for a better experience).

**Dinner:** For something special, book **Mirandus** at Vivenda Miranda — clifftop fine dining with sunset views over Porto de Mós beach. Arrive 30 minutes early for cocktails on the terrace. For something casual, **Nah Nah Bah** does legendary burgers in a tropical tiki atmosphere.

### Day 2: The Benagil Coast — Sea Caves and Cliff Walks

**Drive from Lagos: 40 minutes to Benagil area**

Today you'll explore the most spectacular stretch of coastline in all of Portugal — the central coast between Carvoeiro and Benagil.

**Early Morning: Benagil Cave Boat Tour**

Book the **earliest morning departure** (usually 7:30-8:00am) from Portimão or Carvoeiro. This is the single most important timing hack in this entire guide. At sunrise, you might be the only boat inside Portugal's most famous sea cave — the cathedral-like dome with sunlight streaming through the collapsed ceiling. By 11am, there are 15+ boats queuing to enter.

The tour (1.5-2.5 hours, €25-45 per person) also passes Praia da Marinha and dozens of other coastal caves.

**Mid-Morning: Praia da Marinha**

After the boat tour, drive to Praia da Marinha — consistently rated among Europe's most beautiful beaches.

![The iconic double arch rock formation at Praia da Marinha](/images/beaches/Praia-da-Marinha.jpg)

Arrive before 10am to beat the tour buses. The double arch formation is more dramatic in person than any photo suggests. At low tide, walk along the base of the cliffs for the best views.

**Late Morning: Seven Hanging Valleys Trail**

From the Praia da Marinha car park, walk west along the **Seven Hanging Valleys Trail** — the Algarve's most famous coastal hike. You don't need to do the full 5.7km — even the first 2km offers jaw-dropping views of sea stacks, collapsed cave ceilings, and hidden coves far below.

**Lunch: Cliff Dining**

Two exceptional options:

- **Boneca Bar** at Algar Seco, Carvoeiro — A proper restaurant (despite the name) built right into the dramatic rock formations. Charcoal-grilled fish, terrace views you won't believe. Established in 1969.
- **Rei das Praias** near Ferragudo — A restaurant literally built into the cliff above Praia dos Caneiros, operating since 1976. Book a window table for sunset if you want to come back for dinner instead.

**Afternoon: Carvoeiro and Algar Seco**

Drive to the village of Carvoeiro — a cluster of white houses tumbling down to a tiny beach framed by golden cliffs. Walk the boardwalk trail west to the **Algar Seco** rock formations — otherworldly arches, caves, and natural windows carved by the sea.

![The picturesque village of Carvoeiro nestled between golden cliffs](/images/towns/carvoeiro.jpg)

**Evening: Ferragudo**

Cross to Ferragudo, the prettiest fishing village on the central coast. White-washed houses climb a gentle hill above the Arade River, connected by narrow cobbled lanes.

**Dinner at O Pescador** — Family-run seafood on the quayside. Or **A Ria** along the river — where local fishermen bring in fresh catch from the Arade estuary. Ask for conquilhas (tiny clams) if they're in season.

Watch sunset from Praia Grande — Ferragudo's main beach faces west and golden hour here is spectacular.

### Day 3: Sagres and Cape St. Vincent — The Edge of Europe

**Drive from Lagos/Ferragudo: 30-40 minutes to Sagres**

Your final day heads to the southwestern tip of continental Europe — where Portugal ends and the Atlantic begins.

**Morning: Fortaleza de Sagres**

The fortress where Prince Henry the Navigator planned the voyages that changed the world. The windswept promontory feels like the edge of existence. Arrive when it opens (10am) and spend an hour exploring the grounds — the mysterious wind compass on the ground, the coastal views in every direction, and the sense of history are powerful.

![The windswept fortress of Sagres perched on dramatic cliffs above the Atlantic](/images/towns/sagres.jpg)

**Mid-Morning: Praia do Tonel**

Walk down to Praia do Tonel, the surf beach directly below the fortress. Even if you don't surf, it's worth the descent. The fortress silhouette looming above, the Atlantic crashing against massive cliffs — this is the Algarve at its most raw and dramatic.

If you want to try surfing, book a lesson here (€35-50 per person, 2-3 hours, wetsuit included). The schools in Sagres are run by hardcore surfers who know these waters intimately.

**Lunch: Harbour Dining**

- **A Tasca** at Porto da Baleeira harbour — A family-run tavern since 1959. Grilled fish straight off the boats, honest prices, no pretension. After eating, walk to the harbour wall for views. The combination of cheap food and end-of-the-world atmosphere is hard to beat.
- **A Sagres** — The most reliable seafood in town. Family-run, with fish from the southwestern coast.

**Afternoon: Cape St. Vincent**

Drive 6km west to **Cape St. Vincent** — continental Europe's most southwestern point. The lighthouse, standing since 1846, marks where the known world once ended. The cliffs drop 75 metres to the churning Atlantic below.

The "last bratwurst before America" food truck is parked here — an oddly perfect detail.

**The Sunset: The Main Event**

Stay for sunset at Cape St. Vincent. This is widely considered one of the most dramatic sunsets in Europe — watching the sun sink into the endless Atlantic from what was once believed to be the edge of the world. Arrive by 7pm in summer to secure parking and a good clifftop position.

After sunset, drive back to Lagos (30 minutes) for a final dinner in the old town.

## The 5-Day Algarve Itinerary: Adding Depth

Follow Days 1-3 above, then continue with these two additional days that reveal a completely different side of the Algarve.

### Day 4: The Eastern Algarve — Elegance and Authenticity

**Drive from Lagos: 1 hour to Tavira via A22**

Today you'll discover why seasoned travellers often prefer the eastern Algarve. It's quieter, more Portuguese, and has a completely different character from the dramatic cliffs of the west.

**Morning: Tavira Old Town**

Tavira is everything Lagos isn't — quiet, elegant, and almost entirely Portuguese. More churches per square metre than any town its size should have, a Roman bridge, and a genteel atmosphere that feels like stepping back in time.

![The elegant riverside town of Tavira with its Roman bridge and church spires](/images/towns/tavira.jpg)

Start with coffee at **Pastelaria Tavirense** in the old town. Then explore:

- **Roman Bridge** — Cross the Gilão River on this seven-arched bridge. At sunset it glows golden — but it's beautiful at any hour.
- **Castle ruins** — Climb to the Moorish walls for panoramic views over terracotta rooftops and the Ria Formosa beyond.
- **Camera Obscura tower** — A water tower converted into a 360° live projection of the town. Genuinely fascinating and completely unique.

**Late Morning: Santa Luzia — The Octopus Capital**

Drive 5 minutes west to Santa Luzia, a tiny fishing village known as the octopus capital of Portugal.

**Lunch at Casa do Polvo Tasquinha** — Every dish features polvo (octopus). The polvo à lagareiro is pressure-cooked for hours until impossibly tender, then finished in olive oil with crushed garlic. Listed in the Michelin Guide. **Arrive at 12:30 sharp** — before the tour groups descend around 1pm. Reservations essential.

After lunch, walk the waterfront and watch the octopus pots being hauled in.

**Afternoon: Ilha de Tavira**

Take the ferry from Tavira town centre (€2 return, runs every 15-30 minutes in summer) to **Ilha de Tavira** — a barrier island beach that stretches for kilometres. The water here is warmer than the western coast, the sand is wider, and the Ria Formosa lagoon behind you is teeming with birdlife.

![The endless golden sands of Ilha de Tavira stretching into the distance](/images/beaches/Ilha-de-Tavira.jpg)

**Local tip:** Walk 10 minutes east from the ferry landing and the crowd thins dramatically. Bring a picnic and find your own stretch.

**Evening: Wine and Tapas in Tavira**

Back in Tavira, head to **Mira Tapas & Wine** for Algarvean wine flights and seasonal tapas on the riverside. Friday evenings bring live music. Or for a once-in-a-lifetime dinner, book **A Ver Tavira** — one Michelin star, perched alongside the ancient castle walls with panoramic views over the rooftops and river.

### Day 5: The Interior — Mountains, Castles, and the Best Chicken in Portugal

**Drive from Tavira: 1 hour 15 minutes to Silves via A22**

Most tourists never leave the coast. Today you'll understand why that's a mistake.

**Morning: Silves — The Ancient Moorish Capital**

Before Lisbon was even a thought, Silves was the capital of Moorish Portugal. Its red sandstone castle still dominates the skyline, surrounded by orange groves and the slow-moving Arade River below.

![The red sandstone walls of Silves Castle overlooking the whitewashed town](/images/day-trips/Castelo-de-silves.jpg)

- **Silves Castle** — One of the best-preserved Moorish castles in Portugal. Walk the ramparts for views over the Algarve interior. Limited shade — go in the morning.
- **Silves Cathedral** — Gothic architecture built on the site of a mosque. Atmospheric and rarely crowded.
- **Café Inglês** — Stop here for coffee with a castle-view terrace. The fresh-squeezed orange juice is the real deal (Silves is the orange capital of the Algarve). Their cheese and charcuterie board pairs perfectly with the view.

**Lunch: Two Legendary Options**

- **Marisqueira Rui** — A seafood institution in an inland town since 1977. Sounds wrong, tastes right. The shellfish comes up from the coast daily. Their gambas à Rui (signature prawns) are famous. Reservations essential.
- **O Barradas** — Traditional Algarvian cooking in a converted farmhouse among orange groves. The cataplana is legendary. Ask for a table in the garden terrace.

**Afternoon: Monchique Mountains**

Drive 30 minutes north to the **Serra de Monchique** — the Algarve's mountain escape. At 450m altitude, the air is cooler, the landscape transforms to cork forests and eucalyptus, and the pace drops to zero.

![The mountain village of Monchique surrounded by cork forests and eucalyptus](/images/towns/monchique.jpg)

- **Caldas de Monchique** — A hidden valley spa village with mineral-rich thermal springs. The hamlet itself is charming — walk the shaded paths between the old spa buildings.
- **Fóia peak (902m)** — Drive to the highest point in the Algarve. On a clear day, you can see both the south and west coasts simultaneously. The panoramic view is staggering.
- **Medronho tasting** — Buy a bottle of the local firewater made from wild strawberry tree berries. The family distilleries around Monchique produce the best in Portugal.

**The Piri-Piri Detour**

On the way back to the coast, stop at **Foz do Banho** in Monchique for the Algarve's legendary piri-piri chicken. Or if heading toward Albufeira, detour to **Ramires in Guia** — the most famous piri-piri chicken restaurant in the Algarve, operating since 1964. About €10 for a half chicken with fries and salad.

**Evening:**

Return to the coast. If you're positioning for Day 6, stay in Lagos or the central coast.

## The 7-Day Algarve Itinerary: The Complete Experience

Follow Days 1-5 above, then add these two days that round out the full Algarve experience — from desert islands to wild Atlantic beaches.

### Day 6: Ria Formosa and Faro — Lagoons, Islands, and a Desert Island Lunch

**Drive from Lagos: 1 hour to Faro via A22**

Today explores the Ria Formosa — a protected lagoon system stretching 60km along the eastern coast. It's home to flamingos, seahorses, and pristine barrier island beaches that most tourists never see.

**Morning: Faro Old Town (Cidade Velha)**

Most tourists rush through Faro to catch a transfer. Their loss. Inside the medieval walls, the Cidade Velha is one of the most atmospheric spots in the Algarve.

![Faro Cathedral and its orange tree-lined square inside the medieval old town](/images/towns/faro-cathedral.jpg)

- Enter through the **Arco da Vila** — a neoclassical archway that leads into a cobblestoned time capsule.
- **Faro Cathedral** — Climb the tower for panoramic views over the old town and Ria Formosa lagoon.
- **Bone Chapel (Capela dos Ossos)** — A chapel lined with 1,245 monks' bones. Macabre but unforgettable.

**Late Morning: Ferry to Ilha Deserta**

Take the ferry from Faro's Cais das Portas do Mar (€10 return, 45 minutes, or €20 speedboat for 15 minutes) to **Ilha Deserta** — Portugal's southernmost point. Seven kilometres of white sand, one restaurant, zero buildings. A desert island that's actually real.

**Lunch at O Estamine** — The only restaurant on the entire uninhabited island. Solar-powered, sustainable, and surrounded by nothing but sand, sea, and sky. The fresh fish and arroz de marisco are excellent, but the real luxury is eating with your feet in the sand on a desert island. **Book 2-3 days ahead in summer** — it's the only option and tables fill fast.

Walk 20 minutes from the ferry landing in either direction and it's just you and the ocean. Bring a parasol — there is zero shade on the entire island.

**Afternoon: Olhão Markets and Waterfront**

Ferry back to Faro, then drive 15 minutes east to Olhão — the Algarve's most authentic fishing town.

![The working fishing town of Olhão with its distinctive cubist architecture](/images/towns/olhao.jpg)

The two redbrick market halls are the social centre — one for fish, one for produce. Behind them, a labyrinth of narrow streets leads past flat-roofed cubist houses that look more North African than Portuguese.

If it's a Saturday, the outdoor market takes over the waterfront — produce, cheese, honey, crafts, and people-watching.

**Local tip:** Take the 15-minute ferry from Olhão to **Ilha da Armona** or **Culatra Island** (€3 return) — authentic fishing village communities with no cars and beautiful beaches. A cheaper, more authentic alternative to the tourist boat tours.

**Evening: Faro Dinner**

- **A Venda** — A tiny petiscos (sharing plates) spot that locals fiercely protect. The menu changes daily based on market availability. Saturday is best. Reservations essential.
- **Dois Irmãos** — A Faro institution since 1925. Traditional Portuguese at its most dependable.

### Day 7: The Wild West Coast — Surf, Cliffs, and Untamed Beauty

**Drive from Faro/Lagos: 40 minutes to Aljezur from Lagos**

Your final day heads north of Lagos to the Costa Vicentina — Europe's best-preserved coastline. The manicured resorts give way to wild Atlantic beaches, towering cliffs, and a raw beauty that feels like a different country.

**Morning: Aljezur**

Start in Aljezur, a small town split between a Moorish hilltop quarter crowned by a ruined castle and a newer valley town. The vibe is alternative without trying — yoga retreats, organic cafés, surfers in beat-up vans.

![The wild Atlantic coastline near Aljezur with dramatic cliffs and surf](/images/towns/aljezur.jpg)

Climb to **Aljezur Castle** — the ruins offer 360° views over the valley and coast. Bring a coffee and take your time.

**Mid-Morning: Praia da Arrifana**

Drive 15 minutes to Arrifana — a dramatic crescent beach at the base of towering cliffs. This is serious surf territory, but non-surfers will love the spectacle. The clifftop has several low-key cafés with views that rival anything on the south coast.

![The dramatic cliff amphitheatre at Praia da Arrifana on the wild west coast](/images/beaches/Praia-da-Arrifana.jpg)

**Local tip:** **Pont'a Pé** restaurant in Arrifana village is a local favourite for tapas and wine. It's inside the town rather than on the cliffs, which keeps prices honest and the crowd local.

**Late Morning: Praia da Bordeira**

Drive 20 minutes south to **Praia da Bordeira** — an enormous beach stretching over a kilometre. The river that crosses the sand creates shallow warm pools perfect for wading. Even on the busiest summer weekend, you'll find space here.

**Lunch: Cliff Dining at Carrapateira**

- **Sítio do Forno** — A traditional Portuguese restaurant perched on the cliff overlooking Praia do Amado. The cataplana and grilled fish are both excellent, and the cliff-edge views of surfers riding the waves below are mesmerising.

**Afternoon: Praia do Amado**

Drive 5 minutes to **Praia do Amado** — the Algarve's most consistent surf break. Even if you don't surf, this place has an energy that's hard to explain until you feel it. Wide bay, red-ochre cliffs, and waves that just keep coming.

![Surfers riding the consistent waves at Praia do Amado backed by red-ochre cliffs](/images/beaches/Praia-do-Amado.jpg)

Grab a beer from the snack bar, sit on the cliff, and watch the sunset with the surfers. This is the Algarve at its most untamed — the perfect final image of your trip.

**Alternatively:** If you'd prefer a more relaxed Day 7, head to **Praia da Salema** — Rick Steves' favourite Algarve beach. An authentic fishing village with 150-million-year-old dinosaur footprints at low tide, followed by dinner at **Água na Boca** — one of the finest restaurants in the western Algarve, hidden in one of the region's last unspoiled fishing villages.

## Budget Guide: What to Expect

### Budget Traveller (€50-80/day)
- **Stay:** Hostels (€15-35/night)
- **Eat:** Market picnics, tascas, menu do dia (€15-25/day)
- **Transport:** Shared car or bus (€10-20/day)
- **Activities:** Free beaches and hikes, one paid tour (€10-15/day)

### Mid-Range Comfort (€120-200/day)
- **Stay:** Hotels and guesthouses (€60-120/night)
- **Eat:** Mix of casual and nice restaurants (€40-60/day)
- **Transport:** Rental car (€30-50/day including fuel)
- **Activities:** Boat tours, kayaking, wine tasting (€25-45/day)

### Luxury Experience (€300-500+/day)
- **Stay:** Boutique hotels and villas (€150-400/night)
- **Eat:** Michelin restaurants and wine pairings (€80-150/day)
- **Transport:** Rental car (€50-80/day)
- **Activities:** Private tours, sailing, golf (€50-100/day)

## What to Pack

- **Sunscreen** — The Algarve sun is stronger than it feels, even on cloudy days
- **Layers** — Mountain areas like Monchique are 5-10°C cooler than the coast. Evening boat trips get chilly even in summer
- **Water shoes** — Many cove beaches have rocky shallows
- **Cash** — Some villages, parking meters, and small restaurants don't take cards
- **Comfortable walking shoes** — Cobblestones are everywhere. Save the flip-flops for the beach

## Frequently Asked Questions

### Can I do the Algarve without a car?

Technically yes, but you'll miss 80% of what makes the region special. Buses connect major towns but run infrequently, and the best beaches, viewpoints, and restaurants are off the bus routes. Our strong recommendation: rent a car. Even 3-4 days of rental transforms the experience.

### What's the best base for exploring the Algarve?

**Lagos** for the western coast and dramatic cliff scenery. **Albufeira** for the central coast with easy access everywhere. **Tavira** for the eastern Algarve and Ria Formosa. If you only have one base, Lagos offers the best combination of beaches, restaurants, nightlife, and day trip access.

### Should I go east-to-west or west-to-east?

We recommend **starting in the west** (Lagos) and working east. The western Algarve has the most dramatic scenery, so you start with the biggest "wow" factor. The east offers a different kind of beauty — more refined, more Portuguese — that you'll appreciate more after a few days.

### Is the Algarve expensive?

Less than you'd think. A decent lunch at a local tasca costs €10-15 per person. Piri-piri chicken with fries and salad is about €10. Even mid-range restaurants rarely exceed €30 per person with wine. The biggest costs are accommodation in summer and car rental.

### How far in advance should I book?

**Summer (July-August):** Book accommodation and popular activities (Benagil boat tours, Michelin restaurants) at least 2-4 weeks ahead. Car rental gets expensive if you wait.

**Shoulder season (May-June, September-October):** 1-2 weeks is usually fine for most things. Restaurants still benefit from advance booking on weekends.

**Off-season (November-April):** Minimal booking needed except Michelin restaurants. Some seasonal restaurants and activities close.

### Can I combine the Algarve with Lisbon?

Absolutely. Lisbon is about 2.5-3 hours by car or train from Faro. Many travellers spend 3-4 days in Lisbon before heading south. If you're driving, the A2 motorway connects the two regions directly.
